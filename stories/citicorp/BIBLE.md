# The 59-Story Secret: Master Production Bible

Series: 4 vertical parts (30 to 60 s each) feeding one YouTube long-form master.
Status: Part 1 rendered. Parts 2 to 4 scripted, not yet storyboarded.

## Hook and premise

A skyscraper in Midtown Manhattan could have been blown over, and the people
who knew kept it quiet for almost twenty years. Viewers stop because the first
frame is a tower tilting under the word FELL.

## Fact sheet (verified, safe to say)

| Claim | Status |
|---|---|
| Citicorp Center, Midtown Manhattan, 59 stories, about 915 ft | verified |
| Stands on four stilts at the middle of each side, corners open, because of St. Peter's Lutheran Church; the deal barred columns through the new church | verified |
| 1978: Princeton engineering student Diane Hartley studied the design for her senior thesis; she spoke with a junior engineer in LeMessurier's office, not with him directly | verified |
| LeMessurier's July 1978 calcs: quartering winds raised loads 40% on four of eight chevron tiers and 160% at bolted joints | verified |
| Welds were swapped for bolts to save money (proposed by Bethlehem Steel), accepted by LeMessurier's office; he learned of it later | verified |
| Tuned mass damper needs electricity; without it, a storm able to topple the tower hits NYC about once every 16 years (about 55 with it) | verified |
| Secret night welding of steel plates over 200+ bolted joints, stopping before workers arrived | verified |
| Emergency plans covered roughly a 10-block radius; Red Cross volunteers on standby | verified (headcount disputed, do not state) |
| Hurricane Ella threatened NYC weeks into the repairs, then turned out to sea | verified |
| Story broke in May 1995 in The New Yorker (Joe Morgenstern), who first heard it at a party | verified |
| Hartley later recognized herself as the student after seeing a documentary | verified |

Do not state: repair cost, damper weight, Red Cross headcount, exact repair end
date. Sources disagree on all four.

## Part 1 (rendered, 44 s)

Hook: "This skyscraper almost fell on New York." Ends on the joints, before the
bolts reveal. Full script and beat timing: `part1.json`.

## Part 2 (draft, about 40 s): The bolts

1. [RECAP 2 s] A student's questions. Corner winds. A 160% jump on key joints.
2. The design called for welded joints.
3. During construction, the welds were swapped for bolts to save money. His own office approved it.
4. LeMessurier didn't know.
5. Bolts passed the original math. Not the corner winds.
6. Up top sat a tuned mass damper, a giant block that calms the sway.
7. But the damper needs electricity. Big storms knock out power.
8. Without it, a storm strong enough to topple the tower hits New York about once every sixteen years.
9. And it was summer. Hurricane season.
10. He had two choices. Stay quiet. Or confess.
11. Part three.

Visuals: weld vs bolt split card, damper cutaway, power cable cut, 16 vs 55
year bar, calendar flipping to August, two-door choice.

## Part 3 (draft, about 40 s): Night shift

1. [RECAP 2 s] Bolts, not welds. A 1-in-16-year storm.
2. He confessed to the building's owner.
3. Welders went in at night, after the offices emptied.
4. They welded steel plates over more than two hundred bolted joints.
5. They stopped before anyone came back in the morning.
6. Emergency plans covered about ten blocks around the tower.
7. Then, weeks into the repairs, a hurricane formed. Ella.
8. Heading for New York.
9. The welding wasn't finished.
10. Part four.

Visuals: clock face day/night flips, joint counter 0 to 200+, map with 10-block
radius ring, hurricane track scrubbing up the coast.

## Part 4 (draft, about 40 s): Silence

1. [RECAP 2 s] Half-welded tower. A hurricane coming.
2. Ella turned out to sea.
3. The repairs finished that fall. The tower stood.
4. And for almost twenty years, nobody talked.
5. In 1995, writer Joe Morgenstern heard the story at a party.
6. He broke it in The New Yorker.
7. The article never named the student.
8. Years later, Diane Hartley saw a documentary and realized it was her.
9. One question. One engineer who didn't stay quiet.
10. Full story on YouTube.
